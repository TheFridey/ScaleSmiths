## C07 Finding Prospects (BLOCKED: marketing process)

### C07-L1 Good-fit businesses

Look for established businesses with a clear offer and customers who search before they buy: trades, professional services, health and clinics, local retail, specialist manufacturers. Signals: an outdated or slow site, weak mobile experience, no clear route to enquire, services missing from the site, or an owner who talks about growth. Weaker fit: no budget at all, wants a template, or wants a guarantee.

### C07-L2 Warm introductions

Your own professional network is the best starting point. Introduce people who would genuinely benefit, ask before passing on anyone's details, and be open that you are an independent partner of ScaleSmiths.

### C07-L3 Registering a prospect

Register a prospect in the Partner Portal before detailed conversations. Registration is a preliminary record: duplicates and existing clients are checked, and lead protection applies to ordinary registrations. *[FOUNDER DECISION `AP-COM-06`: the lead protection term, expiry and dispute process are not approved. Do not tell a partner or prospect how long protection lasts.]*

### C07-L4 Researched prospects

*[PROPOSED POLICY]* ScaleSmiths may allocate researched businesses, subject to availability, screening and market approval. No fixed number is guaranteed and nothing is contacted by ScaleSmiths on your behalf. Treat each allocated business as **not assessed for outreach** until the approved process says otherwise.

### C07-L5 Contact rules

*[BLOCKED: approved marketing process, `AP-LEG-03`]* Direct marketing is regulated. For UK business-to-business marketing calls, the ICO directs organisations to check the TPS and CTPS registers and their own do-not-call lists, and electronic marketing rules vary by channel and subscriber type ([ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)). Until ScaleSmiths approves a process, **do not make unsolicited calls or send unsolicited marketing emails on ScaleSmiths' behalf.**

### C07-L6 Suppression and objections

If someone asks not to be contacted, stop, record it in the portal and tell ScaleSmiths. Never argue with an opt-out or try another channel.

### C07-L7 Sectors and exclusivity

Some clients may have sector exclusivity. If the portal shows "commercial checks in progress", that is all you will be told. The details are confidential and decided by founders. Do not guess or ask the prospect about competitors.

## C08 Discovery Meetings

### C08-L1 Preparing

Spend fifteen minutes before the meeting: read their website on a phone, search for their main service and town, note whether they appear, check whether they have a Google Business Profile, and read any reviews. Use [R09 Meeting preparation checklist](resources/R09-meeting-preparation-checklist.md). Note questions, not conclusions.

### C08-L2 Setting the agenda

Open with purpose and time: "I'd like to understand how the business wins customers today and where the website helps or gets in the way. Then, if it makes sense, I'll explain how ScaleSmiths could help and what the next step would be." Confirm how long they have.

### C08-L3 The core questions

Use [R07 Discovery questionnaire](resources/R07-discovery-questionnaire.md). The essentials:

1. What do you sell, and which services matter most commercially?
2. Who are your best customers and how do they find you?
3. What does your current website do well? What frustrates you?
4. What evidence do you have: enquiries, calls, analytics, Search Console?
5. What systems do you use (booking, CRM, email, payments)?
6. Who controls the domain, hosting and website accounts?
7. What would a good result look like in a year?
8. Is there a deadline or trigger?
9. Have you set aside a budget range?

### C08-L4 Listening for fit

- "We look dated / we don't get enquiries from the site" → website project.
- "We have a decent site but nobody finds it" → Business Growth Audit or Growth Partnership.
- "We're not sure what's wrong" → Business Growth Audit.
- "We run everything on spreadsheets / our booking is a mess" → bespoke discovery.
- "Our emails go to spam / we use a Gmail address" → Managed Business Email.

### C08-L5 What not to say

No prices beyond the four published starting prices. No timelines. No rankings, traffic or revenue predictions. No commitments about what is included. No criticism of their current supplier. No mention of your commission.

### C08-L6 Writing up

Write notes the same day in the questionnaire format: facts they told you, your observations (clearly marked as yours), open questions and the agreed next step.

### C08-L7 Next steps

Offer one clear next step: a quote request, the Business Growth Audit (£395), a joint sales meeting, or technical scoping. Confirm it in writing using [R13 Follow-up templates](resources/R13-follow-up-templates.md).

## C09 Presenting ScaleSmiths

### C09-L1 Problem first

Open by playing back what you heard: "You said most new customers come from referrals, and the site doesn't explain your commercial services." Then connect each part of your presentation to one of those points.

### C09-L2 The ScaleSmiths approach

Four points, each from published wording: protect what already works; write the scope down; deliver in phases with a dependable first release; measure against agreed signals instead of invented targets.

### C09-L3 Using published work

You may describe what was built for published clients as the FAQ library and case studies describe it (see C06-L2), once founders approve partner use (`AP-PROOF-01`). Point people to the case studies on the ScaleSmiths website instead of retelling them.

### C09-L4 Proof you must not use

Testimonials or quotes not shown on the site; outcome figures that are not in an approved resource; client names that are not published; "we've helped hundreds of businesses"-style claims; screenshots of private systems.

### C09-L5 Presenting price

State the relevant published starting price and the boundary. Suggest the Business Growth Audit where the need is unclear: it is a one-time review and prioritised roadmap, and where the published terms apply the full fee is credited against an eligible subsequent ScaleSmiths build.

### C09-L6 A respectful close

"Would it help if I arranged a proper quote?" is enough. Agree a date to follow up. Never use false urgency ("this price ends Friday").

## C10 Objection Handling

The full set of responses, with approval status for each, is in [R08 Objection-handling playbook](resources/R08-objection-handling-playbook.md).

### C10-L1 The pattern

1. **Acknowledge** the concern without arguing.
2. **Clarify** what is behind it with a question.
3. **Answer** with approved wording.
4. **Confirm** whether that helps, and agree the next step.

### C10-L2 When to stop

Stop if the business is not a good fit, if the person is under pressure or vulnerable, or if the objection needs ScaleSmiths (contract terms, technical risk, a complaint about a previous supplier, a legal question). Saying "that's a question for ScaleSmiths; I'll get you a proper answer" is a good outcome.

### C10-L3 "It is too expensive"

Clarify: compared with what, and for what scope? Explain that a starting price is an entry point for an agreed scope, that proposals list what is included, that work can be phased, and that the Business Growth Audit is a lower-cost first step. **Never offer a discount.** Discount allowances are disabled and only founders can reserve one *[PROPOSED POLICY]*.

### C10-L4 "We already have a website or an agency"

Respect the existing relationship. ScaleSmiths starts by reviewing what currently works. An audit can serve as an independent second opinion, and its findings are useful whoever implements them.

### C10-L5 "Can you guarantee results?"

No. Use the approved line from C03-L4. Explain that commitments are to the work and to measuring against a baseline.

### C10-L6 "We use WordPress, Wix or Shopify"

ScaleSmiths is not anti-platform. It looks at what the current platform is doing well before recommending whether a rebuild is justified, and where an established platform fits, configuring it may be the better decision.

### C10-L7 "We need to think about it"

Agree, summarise what you discussed, offer to send a short written summary, and agree when to follow up.

### C10-L8 Using the objection workspace

Search for the objection in the client's words, read the partner guidance, then copy only the **client-facing** line. Practise responses aloud. Report new objections to ScaleSmiths so an approved answer can be written.

## C11 Commercial Policies (BLOCKED: agreement and terms)

> Every partner-term figure in this course is **PROPOSED POLICY**: an illustration of the proposed programme, not an approved agreement. Plans are disabled by default. No reward is payable. This course must not be published until the decisions listed in [CURRICULUM.md](CURRICULUM.md) are recorded.

### C11-L1 Published starting prices

Website from £2,000; Growth Partnership from £350 per month; Business Growth Audit £395; business email from £15 per month. GBP, and always with the starting-price boundary.

### C11-L2 Proposals and payment terms

**Source:** faq:deposit. Payment terms, including any staged or upfront payment, are set in each proposal and signed order. There is no blanket published deposit policy. Never describe a payment schedule to a client.

### C11-L3 How commission is proposed to work

*[PROPOSED POLICY]* Source: `web/src/lib/partner-programme.ts`, [COMMISSION-RULES.md](../COMMISSION-RULES.md).

- Commission is proposed at **30% of eligible payments actually collected**, VAT-exclusive, under the final partner agreement.
- For a standard website plan it applies to the build fee and the **first** Growth Partnership payment only. Later monthly payments earn no acquisition commission.
- Only payments a ScaleSmiths founder has verified as collected count. A pending contract value is never earned.
- Under the current plans (`standard-v2`, `growth-only-v1`, `bespoke-v1`), build payments are pooled and rounded once on the cumulative amount, so splitting payments cannot increase the total. Historical `standard-v1` agreements keep their original per-milestone rounding, which can differ by a few pence.
- Refunds do not reduce commission automatically; they are held for founder review, and any reversal is a recorded, approved entry with a reason (final refund treatment is undecided).

### C11-L4 Worked examples

*[PROPOSED POLICY, accountant review required]*

| Scenario | Client payments (ex VAT) | Proposed commission |
|---|---|---|
| Standard website + first Growth Partnership payment | £2,000 + £350 | £600 + £105 = **£705** |
| Discounted build at £1,500 (founder-approved only) | £1,500 + £350 | £450 + £105 = **£555** |
| Growth Partnership only | first £350 | **£105** |
| Bespoke project, £10,000 eligible collected | £10,000 | **£3,000** (30% unless a different approved rate) |

Payment order (deposit then balance, or equal instalments) does not change the total. Commission becomes due only as payments are collected and verified. These are illustrations, not earnings forecasts: nothing is earned until a client pays and a founder verifies the payment.

**Historical agreements.** Agreements accepted under the earlier `standard-v1` plan (a six-month Growth Partnership term, £4,100 minimum contract value) keep the terms under which they were accepted. The current proposed standard is the twelve-month `standard-v2` term (£6,200 minimum contract value). Never quote the six-month term for a new client, and never describe either term to a client: terms come from ScaleSmiths' proposal.

### C11-L5 What is not decided

Withdrawal frequency, minimum and invoicing; partner VAT treatment; refund and clawback treatment; achievement rewards (planned, not payable); the partner referral reward (planned, not payable); market approval (the software starts with no market approved, including the UK, and approves markets only when a founder records it); discount allowances (none approved).

### C11-L6 What you may and may not agree

You may: share the published starting prices, explain the starting-price boundary, and arrange a quote. You may not: agree a price, discount, payment schedule, timeline, scope or contract term, or describe your commission to a client.

### C11-L7 Markets

All prices are GBP. No international pricing, tax treatment or currency conversion exists. A customer's market must be approved by founders before a contract can be accepted.

## C12 Professional Conduct (BLOCKED: agreement and solicitor review)

### C12-L1 Independent, not employed

*[PROPOSED POLICY, employment solicitor review `AP-GOV-02`]* The partnership is an independent, commission-only arrangement. There is no salary, guaranteed income, compulsory working hours or sales target. You choose whether to use the Academy. Any future employed role would be a separate offer with its own agreement, and nothing about that is guaranteed.

### C12-L2 How to describe yourself

Use the approved title once decided (`AP-GOV-05`; the draft programme uses "Independent Business Partner"). Say "I'm an independent partner of ScaleSmiths", not "I work for ScaleSmiths". Never imply that you are an employee, director, owner or shareholder.

### C12-L3 Truthful representation

Use approved content only. No guarantees, invented proof, unapproved prices or claims about features that are planned or in development. If you are unsure, say you will check.

### C12-L4 Pressure and vulnerability

No pressure selling, false urgency or repeated contact after a "no". Take extra care if someone seems confused, distressed or not in a position to make a business decision; stop and tell ScaleSmiths.

### C12-L5 Personal data

*[Privacy adviser review `AP-PRIV-01`]* Collect only what is needed to register and discuss an opportunity. Record it in the Partner Portal, not in personal spreadsheets or messaging apps. Never ask a client for passwords; ScaleSmiths uses delegated or scoped access and never asks for registrar passwords through a public form (faq:dns).

### C12-L6 Confidentiality

Commission terms, other partners, exclusivity, internal resources marked "Partner only" and anything a founder shares in confidence stay private.

### C12-L7 Complaints and mistakes

If a client complains, or you realise you said something wrong, tell ScaleSmiths the same day through an **escalation** meeting request. Correcting a mistake early is always better than covering it.

## C13 Advanced and Enterprise Opportunities

### C13-L1 Established business transformation

**Source:** `engagement-models.ts`. Multi-site and established organisations that have outgrown a brochure site: customer journeys, integrations and operational efficiency across a digital estate.

### C13-L2 Custom platforms and enterprise

Bespoke architecture, workflows, security, migrations and support for complex platforms. See the `/enterprise` and `/enterprise-websites` pages for the published positioning.

### C13-L3 Scoped after discovery

Complex work is never sold against a starting price. Do not suggest a range. ScaleSmiths runs discovery and writes the scope.

### C13-L4 Existing clients and additional work

*[PROPOSED POLICY]* Additional projects for an existing ScaleSmiths client are handled through a founder-reviewed additional-business claim. What counts as documented partner contribution (introduction record, meeting notes, joint sign-off) is an open founder decision.

### C13-L5 Preparing a bespoke discovery request

Organisation and sites; the decision-makers; the systems and integrations involved; the business problem and its cost today; compliance or security requirements mentioned; timing and procurement process.

### C13-L6 Joint meetings

Request a **joint sales** meeting when a prospect is ready to discuss detail. Brief ScaleSmiths beforehand with your notes. In the meeting, let ScaleSmiths answer scope, technical and commercial questions.
