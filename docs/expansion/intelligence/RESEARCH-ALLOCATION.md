# Researched prospect allocation (checkpoint 5, migration 0084)

A **candidate** is founder-held research about a business found from a public source. It is not a lead, an enquiry or a qualified or interested customer, and nothing in the system contacts the business.

- **Record:** business name, website and normalised domain, industry, canonical sector, country, region, area, public source, research date, partner-visible notes and founder-only notes.
- **Gates, evaluated live and again at allocation:** existing client, existing prospect or partner registration or other candidate, suppression, market approval, sector exclusivity (candidate-level founder clearance for review matches only; a confirmed restriction is never clearable), and marketing screening, which needs a founder's recorded evidence and is **never inferred or completed automatically**.
- **Lifecycle:** draft, eligible, allocated, excluded. Allocation creates or revives the canonical `prospects` row and `expansion_opportunities` row, so the partner works in the one existing CRM workflow. Release withdraws an unworked prospect and returns the candidate to eligible. Reassignment moves an unworked prospect. A database trigger forbids any other change of an opportunity's partner, and forbids any move once work is recorded.
- **History:** `expansion_candidate_events` is append-only (created, edited, checked, screening, eligible, exclusivity cleared, allocated, released, reassigned, excluded).
- **Partners see:** their own allocated prospects, the public source, research date, sector, location and partner-visible notes, under a notice that it is not a lead. They never see founder notes, check outcomes, exclusivity or other partners' allocations. A partner can still register their own prospects.
- **Volume:** the programme intends roughly 20 to 30 researched businesses per partner, subject to availability. No cap or guarantee is built in or promised.
- **Races proven on real PostgreSQL (separate connections, restricted admin role):** two founders allocating one candidate, duplicate requests, allocation against independent registration, existing client and existing prospect conflicts, suppression, market suspension and exclusivity introduced during allocation, release against reassignment, a worked prospect, revoked partners, unauthorised access and partner A against partner B.
